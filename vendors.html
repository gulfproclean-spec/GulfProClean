What every vendor must have

We verify all of this before a single work order goes out, and we re-verify at every renewal. A vendor whose coverage lapses comes off the dispatch list that day — not as a matter of paperwork tidiness, but because an uninsured subcontractor on a customer's property becomes our liability and our customer's problem.

  • A current Florida license for your trade, where one is required — verified by us directly against the issuing authority (DBPR, FDACS, DOH or the State Fire Marshal), not from a photo of a card.
  • General liability insurance, with Gulf ProClean named as a certificate holder, and preferably as an additional insured. Send the ACORD certificate.
  • Workers' compensation coverage, or a valid Florida exemption certificate. Construction-trade vendors need coverage from the first employee; non-construction vendors from the fourth. If a subcontractor has no coverage, its workers become the hiring contractor's employees for injury purposes — which is why we will not skip this.
  • Commercial auto insurance covering vehicles driven to job sites.
  • A completed IRS Form W-9 so we can issue a 1099 where required.
  • A written rate card — hourly, trip or diagnostic fee, common flat-rate jobs, after-hours and emergency multipliers, and materials markup.
  • Response-time commitment for routine, urgent and emergency calls, and whether you cover storm-season emergencies.
  • Background-screened technicians for any vendor entering an occupied home. We will ask what your screening standard is.
Mold work has its own bar. Florida requires mold assessors and mold remediators to be separately licensed under Chapter 468, Part XVI. Remediators must carry at least $1,000,000 in general liability with specific coverage for mold-related claims; assessors must carry $1,000,000 in general liability plus errors & omissions. The same firm may not both assess and remediate the same project. We hold vendors to all three of those.

How it goes

From submission to first work order

  1. You submit your pricing

    The form takes about fifteen minutes. License number, coverage, service area, response times, and your rate card.

  2. We verify

    We check your license directly with the issuing authority and confirm your insurance is current. Usually two to three business days.

  3. Documents

    We request your certificate of insurance, workers' comp certificate or exemption, and W-9 by email. Nothing sensitive goes through the web form.

  4. Terms conversation

    A short call: rates, payment terms, how dispatch works, what our customers expect on site.

  5. On the bench

    You go into the dispatch list for your trade and service area. Work orders come from our vendor coordinator with the property, the finding, and the access arrangement.

  6. Ongoing

    We track responsiveness, quality, callbacks and pricing accuracy, and we re-verify your license and insurance at each renewal. Lapsed coverage pauses dispatch until it is current again.
